<Chapter number="01" label="The brief">
  Mercury came to us with a single sentence: "We want to talk to people who work odd hours." No script, no visuals, no hero product shot. Two weeks for a treatment, three to shoot, two to cut. They wanted a film, not a commercial.
</Chapter>

<Chapter number="02" label="The idea">
  Rather than a montage of jobs, we built the film around a single thread: the hour between 3 and 4 a.m. The bakers pulling the first rack. The dispatchers taking the last call before the shift change. The cleaners moving through offices that will be empty for another four hours. One hour, told in thirty small frames — the point of view of someone who is also awake, paying attention, not asking anything of them.

</Chapter>

<Chapter number="03" label="Pre-production">
  Storyboards were drawn against real locations, not fantasy ones. We scouted 19 and picked 11. Every shot had to be achievable with a three-person crew because the budget made us choose between talent fees and a larger team, and we chose talent.

<Chapter number="04" label="On set">
  Five people, one van. Alexa Mini LF with a set of Cooke S4s, mostly available light augmented with a single 300W LED panel when a scene needed a lift. Day two we lost a location at 2 a.m. when a manager changed his mind, and we rebuilt the schedule inside a diner with the producer over a second cup of coffee. The final edit cut that night's footage down to 40 seconds and it is still the part of the film I rewatch the most.
