I've been making films and photographs for a decade. The work started narrowly — documentary shorts on a borrowed 5D, printed in small batches for friends — and has widened since to include commercial film for agencies, architecture photography for institutions, and, more recently, small pieces of software that solve problems the previous two disciplines taught me to see.

The through-line is attention. A director's job is the same as a photographer's and the same as a developer's: decide what matters in a frame, in a sequence, or in a system — then remove everything that doesn't serve it. Most briefs arrive with too many ideas, and most cuts get better when you delete the second-best shot. I try to keep both things in mind.

Based on the East Coast, happy to travel. Projects tend to be small crews, real locations, and short turnarounds. If you want to see more work than the homepage shows, [the work index](/work) has the full set.
